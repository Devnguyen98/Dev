# Standards, certifications and regulatory requirements for exporting areca palm leaf (mo cau) food-contact tableware/packaging from Vietnam (status as of Nov 2026)

> Method note: research was done with web search only. Direct page fetches to fda.gov, accessdata.fda.gov and eur-lex.europa.eu failed because those hosts are blocked in this session's network settings. Findings for those sources come from search-result summaries and secondary sources (SGS, law firms, trade press). Primary texts should be re-checked before any compliance decision. Items marked "(general knowledge, unverified)" are from background knowledge, not from a source fetched in this session.

## Q1. United States: FDA food-contact status, state compostable-labelling laws, PFAS, BPI/ASTM, import alerts

### Takeaway
**This is the biggest finding and it can stop a US launch.** Since May 2025 the US FDA has treated areca catechu palm-leaf dinnerware as not GRAS (not "generally recognised as safe") for food contact. The products are on **Import Alert 23-15**, which lets the FDA hold shipments at the border without inspecting them (DWPE, "detention without physical examination"). The FDA lists no food additive regulation, food contact notification (FCN) or Threshold of Regulation exemption for them. In practice the US is closed to mo cau food-contact products unless someone obtains an FCN. That is a major regulatory project, not a routine test. The state compostable-labelling laws and BPI only matter once that is solved.

### Cited Findings
- On 8 May 2025 the FDA wrote to retailers, distributors and importers about dinnerware (bowls, plates, cups, cutlery) made from the leaf sheath of *Areca catechu*. It said the products can leach toxic chemicals. It found no food additive regulation, effective FCN or TOR exemption covering them. — [FDA constituent update](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-alerts-industry-and-consumers-about-palm-leaf-dinnerware); [FDA letter](https://www.fda.gov/food/post-market-determinations-use-substance-not-gras/letter-retailers-distributors-and-importers-dinnerware)
- The FDA found four naturally occurring alkaloids migrating into a food simulant under intended-use conditions: arecoline, arecaidine, guvacoline and guvacine. One of them is a known carcinogen, and the same alkaloids occur in areca (betel) nut. — [SGS SafeGuardS 079/25](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2025/06/safeguards-07925-us-fda-issues-letter-to-industry-and-consumers-about-palm-leaf-dinnerware); [FDA research memo (media/186355)](https://www.fda.gov/media/186355/download)
- One secondary source says migration was higher when the products were microwaved. — [Restaurantware blog](https://www.restaurantware.com/blogs/news-updates/fda-bans-palm-leaf-dinnerware-over-food-safety-concerns) (secondary source)
- The FDA added its evaluation to the public inventory "Post-market Determinations that the Use of a Substance is Not GRAS". — [Garg Law](https://garg-law.com/fda-alerts-industry-and-consumers-about-palm-leaf-dinnerware/)
- Import Alert 23-15 covers foodware made from *A. catechu* leaves and allows DWPE. — [FDA Import Alert 23-15](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_IA/importalert_1139.html)
- The FDA said the concern is from long-term use and is not acute, so it did not ask retailers to recall products already sold. — [FDA constituent update](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-alerts-industry-and-consumers-about-palm-leaf-dinnerware)
- According to SGS, an operator that wants to sell these products in the US may submit a Food Contact Notification. — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2025/06/safeguards-07925-us-fda-issues-letter-to-industry-and-consumers-about-palm-leaf-dinnerware)
- A refused shipment must be destroyed or re-exported within 90 days of the Refusal Notice. A refusal is final unless it was issued in error. — [FDA Import Refusals](https://www.fda.gov/industry/fda-import-process/import-refusals)
- Reported effects on Indian exporters, the main supplier origin, are covered in trade press. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fus-fda-toxic-tag-to-areca-foodware-hits-exporters-3559904); [KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/exportimports/us-fda-issues-import-alert-on-areca-leaf-dinnerware-impacting-indian-eco-exports)
- Vendor sites claim areca shipments are still clearing US customs. These claims are unverified and conflict with the import alert. — [Arbhu Enterprises](https://arbhuenterprises.com/palm-leaf-dinnerware-import-alert-alternatives/); [Anzhu Craft](https://www.anzhucraft.com/palm-leaf-plates-banned-in-usa-fda-rules-alternatives/)
- **California AB 1201 (compostable labelling):** it was due to take effect 1 Jan 2026. It ties "compostable" claims to the product being an allowable organic input under USDA NOP (National Organic Program) rules. On 11 June 2025 CalRecycle sent a letter to BPI that effectively delays this requirement, reportedly to mid-2027. Industry groups were seeking a legislative fix in the 2026 session. — [Resource Recycling, Jun 2025](https://resource-recycling.com/recycling/2025/06/17/california-extends-compostable-labeling-law/); [Packaging Law](https://www.packaginglaw.com/index%2Ephp/news/californias-compostable-labeling-requirement-delayed-varying-compostable-requirements-states); [BPI "Fix AB1201"](https://bpiworld.org/fix-ab1201); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/californias-strict-compostable-packaging-requirements-effective-january-2026)
- **California SB 343 (recyclability claims, including the chasing-arrows symbol):** it applies to products manufactured on or after 4 Oct 2026. A federal court has paused enforcement of parts of it, but the implementation date itself was not suspended. — [Nixon Peabody, Apr 2026](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/-/media/files/alerts/2026/04/client_alert_re_sb.pdf); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-CAs-Truth-in-Recycling-Law-Compliance-Deadline-Remains-Despite-Federal-Courts-Pause-on-Enforcement)
- **Washington (RCW 70A.455):** a product labelled "compostable" and sold in WA must meet ASTM D6400, D6868 or D8410, ISO 17088 or EN 13432. Food service products must be third-party tested and certified. The labelling requirements have applied since 1 Jan 2024. The law also restricts green, beige or brown tinting and leaf/vine motifs on non-compliant products. — [RCW 70A.455.040](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/RCW/RCW%20%2070A%20TITLE/RCW%20%2070A.455%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2070A.455%20.040.htm); [WA Ecology](https://ecology.wa.gov/waste-toxics/reducing-recycling-waste/plastics/compost-labeling); [WA Ecology blog Jun 2024](https://ecology.wa.gov/blog/june-2024/organics-management-in-washington-changes-to-compostable-product-labeling)
- **BPI fees:** BPI's own pages are inconsistent about the application fee. One FAQ gives $1,500 for a new certification ($500 non-refundable). Another gives $1,500 for commercial-only and $3,000 for commercial plus home. Adding home certification to an existing certificate costs $1,500. Recertification is every 3 years at $1,000 per certificate. The base licensing fee is $3,500 and covers up to 5 certificates. Lab testing is billed separately. — [BPI Fees & Timeline](https://bpiworld.org/fees-timeline); [BPI Certification FAQ](https://bpiworld.org/certification-faq); [BPI New Certification](https://bpiworld.org/new-certification)
- BPI has a "Certification Mark Use Exemptions" page that lists Palm Leaf Foodservice Ware among the exempt categories. The scope of the exemption was not verified. — [BPI exemptions](https://bpiworld.org/certification-mark-use-exemptions)
- US retailers sell palm-leaf ware labelled "No PFAS Added" and "Compostable" (for example the VerTerra lines). — [WebstaurantStore listing](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/verterra-9-large-square-no-pfas-added-compostable-palm-leaf-plate-case/95A012025B.html)

### Inferences
- The legal block for US sales is the GRAS/FCN status, not the lack of a test report. Third-party tests showing low heavy metals or "PFAS-free" do not override Import Alert 23-15. Getting a product removed from DWPE would normally need an FCN, or evidence that the product is outside the alert's scope (for example, not *A. catechu*). An FCN needs a full migration and toxicology dossier and, in general knowledge, costs tens to hundreds of thousands of USD and takes 120 days of FDA review after a complete submission (general knowledge, unverified).
- Recommendation: treat the US as a "no-go" market for mo cau food-contact items in the 2026-2027 plan. Use EU, Japan, Australia and domestic markets, or rethink the product. Possible options are non-food-contact uses, or a validated de-alkaloid treatment followed by an FCN.
- If the US is ever reopened, these state-level rules apply. Washington requires third-party D6400/D6868 certification (BPI) for food-service "compostable" claims. California's AB 1201 date is moving (mid-2027 or later). Many states also have PFAS-in-food-packaging bans, so "No PFAS added" should be backed by total-fluorine testing.

### Gaps
- Whether Import Alert 23-15 has been revised or has a green list as of Nov 2026, and whether any Vietnamese firms are named. The primary FDA page could not be fetched.
- Whether anyone has filed an FCN for areca leaf ware since May 2025.
- The final status of AB 1201 after California's 2026 legislative session.
- The exact scope of BPI's "palm leaf" exemption, and whether BPI still certifies areca products after the FDA action.
- A list of state PFAS bans for food packaging. Not researched in depth for lack of time.

## Q2. EU: 1935/2004, GMP 2023/2006, LFGB, SUP Directive, EN 13432/TÜV OK compost, PPWR, EUDR, RASFF

### Takeaway
The EU has no harmonised specific measure for plant-based tableware. Mo cau products fall under the general safety rule in Article 3 of Regulation 1935/2004 and GMP Regulation 2023/2006, applied nationally (for example Germany's LFGB plus BfR recommendations). Uncoated, chemically unmodified leaf generally falls outside the "plastic" definition of the SUP Directive, so it is not covered by the SUP plate ban. Any plastic, PLA or bio-coating brings the product back inside the ban. PPWR (Regulation (EU) 2025/40) has applied since **12 August 2026**. Its PFAS limits apply to all food-contact packaging, with no stock-exhaustion period. The FDA's alkaloid finding is a known hazard that EU importers and authorities can raise, so arecoline-type migration testing is advisable for EU buyers too.

### Cited Findings
- Plant-based tableware has no specific EU safety rules. A 2021 report noted that, unlike plastics, the safety of these materials is unregulated at EU level. — [BEUC report via vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/sites/default/files/2021-05/Report_BEUC_Towards%20safe%20and%20sustainable%20FCM_0.pdf)
- A 2021 BEUC-coordinated test included 16 palm-leaf bowls and plates. 53% of all sampled products (all materials combined) contained one or more chemicals of concern above recommended levels. — [BEUC/vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/sites/default/files/2021-05/Report_BEUC_Towards%20safe%20and%20sustainable%20FCM_0.pdf); [FoodIngredientsFirst](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/consumer-tests-reveal-toxic-chemicals-in-non-plastic-disposable-tableware-eu-targets-overdue-reform.html)
- RASFF covers food contact materials, and Germany's national contact point is BVL. No areca-specific RASFF notification was found. — [BVL RASFF](https://www.bvl.bund.de/EN/Tasks/01_Food/01_tasks/04_Alert_and_information_systems/00_Alert_and_information_systems/01_RASFF/Im_RASFF_node.html); [EC newsroom on FCM interceptions](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/sante/newsletter-archives/42226)
- A German supplier says its palm-leaf ware is tested to LFGB and BfR recommendations. This is a supplier claim, and it shows what German buyers expect. — [biologischverpacken.de](https://www.biologischverpacken.de/en/suitability-for-food-contact)
- **SUP Directive (EU) 2019/904:** "plastic" excludes "natural polymers that have not been chemically modified", which is section 2.1.3 of the Commission guidelines adopted 31 May 2021. Plates made of paper or bagasse without plastic are allowed. Paper plates with a plastic coating, including a biodegradable or compostable one, are banned. — [Commission SUP guidelines (MITECO copy)](https://www.miteco.gob.es:443/content/dam/miteco/es/calidad-y-evaluacion-ambiental/publicaciones/c20213762finalguidelines_single-use_plastics_products_tcm30-527113.pdf); [European Bioplastics](https://www.european-bioplastics.org/policy/single-use-plastics-directive/)
- The Commission opened an evaluation and public consultation on the SUP Directive on 23 Dec 2025. A 2026 Official Journal notice on plastic definitions exists (C/2026/4813), but its content could not be verified. — [European Bioplastics](https://www.european-bioplastics.org/policy/single-use-plastics-directive/); [EUR-Lex C/2026/4813](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2026/4813/oj/eng)
- **PPWR general application from 12 Aug 2026.** Food-contact packaging above the PFAS limits can no longer be placed on the market, and there is no general stock-exhaustion period. — [European Commission news](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-packaging-enter-application-2026-08-11_en); [Food Packaging Forum](https://foodpackagingforum.org/news/ppwr-pfas-limits-apply-from-august-12)
- PFAS limits: 25 ppb for any individual PFAS (targeted analysis) and 250 ppb for the sum of PFAS. The third limit is described as 50 ppm, either as "total PFAS including polymeric" or as "total fluorine" depending on the source. There is no harmonised test method. The Commission recommends a tiered testing approach. — [Food Packaging Forum](https://foodpackagingforum.org/news/ppwr-pfas-limits-apply-from-august-12); [Intertek bulletin 2026](https://www.intertek.com/products-retail/insight-bulletins/2026/1536-eu-ppwr-requirements-effective-from-august/); [food-safety.com](https://food-safety.com/articles/10087-eu-regulation-limits-pfas-in-food-packaging-bans-single-use-plastic-packaging-for-produce)
- **PPWR Article 9 (compostable):** from 12 Feb 2028, permeable tea/coffee/beverage bags, sticky fruit labels and soft single-serve units must be industrially compostable. Member States may require more. EN 13432 (or an equivalent) is the reference standard. Standards bodies were asked to update harmonised specifications by 12 Feb 2026, and completion of that work was not confirmed. The Commission's PPWR guidance (C/2026/3084) has a section on presumption of conformity for compostable packaging. — [PPWRconnect](https://ppwrconnect.com/blog/ppwr-compostable-packaging-article-9); [European Bioplastics](https://www.european-bioplastics.org/article-9-ppwr-why-certain-packaging-formats-should-be-compostable-2/); [EUR-Lex C/2026/3084](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2026/3084/oj/eng)
- PPWR timeline overview (labelling, recyclability and other obligations phased 2026-2030+). — [FoodNavigator, Aug 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/08/12/ppwr-timeline/); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-summary-of-provisions-and-new-guidance); [Campden BRI](https://www.campdenbri.co.uk/blogs/ppwr-eu-key-changes.php)
- **TÜV Austria OK compost HOME/INDUSTRIAL:** the fees are not public. They are set in the certification contract and vary by product, and lab testing is charged separately. The application process is the same for HOME and INDUSTRIAL. — [ComplianceGate](https://compliancegate.com/ok-compost); [TÜV Austria OK compost HOME](https://en.tuv.at/ok-compost-home-en/)
- **EU plant health (Reg. 2019/2072):** since 14 Dec 2019 all plants and living plant parts need a phytosanitary certificate unless they are listed in Annex XI Part C. Fumigation or heat treatment is required only for specific pests and products. — [EC plant health trade](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/trade-plants-plant-products-non-eu-countries_en); [ComplianceGate fumigation](https://compliancegate.com/fumigation-regulations-european-union)

### Inferences
- EU market checklist: (1) a Declaration of Compliance under Art. 3 of 1935/2004 with a GMP 2023/2006 file, covering traceability (Art. 17), raw-material control and a documented heat-press process. (2) Test reports as German and other buyers expect, to LFGB §30/31 and BfR XXXVI-type tests: overall migration, sensory test (odour/taste transfer), heavy metals (Pb, Cd, As, Hg), formaldehyde if any adhesive is used, PAH, pesticide residues, and mould/microbial counts. (3) **New risk factor:** a migration test for arecoline, arecaidine, guvacoline and guvacine, using a method like the FDA's, to answer buyer due diligence questions after the FDA action. (4) PFAS tiered testing (total fluorine, then targeted) under PPWR. (5) Optional EN 13432 / OK compost INDUSTRIAL or HOME, needed only if the product makes compostability claims.
- For the SUP Directive, keep the product uncoated and free of additives or binders that chemically modify the material, so it stays outside the "plastic" definition. Any PLA, PE or bio-lacquer coating, or a compostable plastic lid, makes the item a SUP-banned plate or a "contains plastic" marked item. Mo cau boxes used as food containers (not plates) also fall outside the SUP container rules if they contain no plastic.
- EUDR (Regulation 2023/1115, as amended by 2024/3234 and further 2025 amendments) covers cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soy and wood and their derived products. *Areca catechu* is not oil palm (*Elaeis guineensis*), and a leaf sheath is not wood, so areca leaf ware is very likely out of scope. The HS heading should still be checked against Annex I (general knowledge, unverified). Under the 2025 amendments, application for large operators is postponed to 30 Dec 2026 (general knowledge, unverified).
- Phytosanitary rules: heat-pressed leaf ware is a processed plant product and is usually not covered by EU phytosanitary certificate requirements, but importers often still ask for a fumigation certificate. Confirm the HS code (often 4602 or 4823 is used in practice, but classification varies) with the Vietnamese Plant Protection Department (PPD) and the EU importer.

### Gaps
- No RASFF notification or BfR opinion specific to areca leaf ware was found, and RASFF Window could not be searched directly.
- The exact text of C/2026/4813 (SUP plastic definition) and whether it names palm leaf.
- TÜV Austria certification fees. These are not public, so a quote is needed.
- Whether EN 13432 harmonised standards were adopted under PPWR by Feb 2026.
- Test costs at SGS, Intertek or Bureau Veritas Vietnam: no published price lists were found. Rough industry ranges from general knowledge, unverified and needing a quote: LFGB basic package (sensory, metals, overall migration) about USD 300-800 per product; a PFAS total-fluorine screen about USD 150-400; targeted PFAS panel about USD 400-1,000; EN 13432 full test (disintegration, biodegradation, ecotoxicity, chemistry) about EUR 5,000-15,000 over 6-12 months, because biodegradation takes up to 6 months.

## Q3. Japan, Korea, Australia

### Takeaway
Japan's Food Sanitation Act positive list covers only synthetic resins. Natural leaf ware falls under the general specifications of Article 18 and the import notification process at quarantine stations. Korea requires MFDS import declaration and foreign-facility registration for utensils, containers and packages. Australia has no specific food-contact regulator for these products. AS 4736 and AS 5810 compostability certification is voluntary (run by ABA). Biosecurity import conditions are checked in BICON.

### Cited Findings
- Japan's positive list (in force since June 2020) covers synthetic-resin apparatus, containers and packaging only. — [Tokyo Metropolitan Gov.](https://www.hokeniryo1.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/shokuhin/eng/kaisei/kiguyoukihousou.html); [MHLW pamphlet](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/english/policy/health-medical/food/dl/pamphlet.pdf); [QIMA update](https://www.qima.com/blog/regulatory-update/regulatory-update-japan-food-contact-materials-list-update)
- Japan sets specifications and standards for utensils, containers and packaging under Article 18 of the Food Sanitation Act. Imported apparatus and containers need an import notification. — [MHLW](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/english/topics/foodsafety/containers/dl/03.pdf); [MHLW import procedures](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/english/topics/importedfoods/11/11-07.html); [trade.gov Japan food packaging](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/japan-food-packaging)
- Korea regulates utensils, containers and packages under its Food Sanitation Act through MFDS "Standards and Specifications for Utensils, Containers and Packages". The English version was revised 27 Jun 2025, but only the Korean text is legally binding. MFDS Notice No. 2026-24 (Mar 2026) updated the standards, mostly for plastics and recycled materials. Import declaration is required, and foreign facilities must register with MFDS before the declaration. — [MFDS English](https://mfds.go.kr/eng/brd/m_15/view.do?seq=72452); [SGS Apr 2026](https://www.sgs.com/en-pt/news/2026/04/safeguards-05526-south-korea-updates-standards-and-specifications-for-food-contact-materials); [TÜV Rheinland](https://tuv.com/ae-ar/market-access-services/regulations-and-standards/south-korea-mfds-notice-no-2026-009-administrative-notice-of-partial-revision-of-19973)
- Australia: BICON states whether a commodity is permitted and whether it needs a permit, documents or treatment. A plant species not listed cannot be imported until a risk analysis is done. — [BICON](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/online-services/bicon); [Managing biosecurity risk – plant products](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/import/goods/plant-products/managing-biosecurity-risk)

### Inferences
- Japan: the importer files the notification. First shipments are usually tested by a registered lab against the general specs (heavy metals, plus any specific tests the station orders). Japanese buyers also commonly ask for formaldehyde, fluorescent whitener and mould tests (general knowledge, unverified). Japan has not announced any areca-alkaloid action, but importers are likely aware of the FDA move.
- Korea: the importer's declaration may trigger an inspection, and the MFDS-registered lab tests the general specs for utensils, containers and packages. Korea has categories for wood and natural-material utensils (general knowledge, unverified), so check which category applies to leaf ware.
- Australia: AS 4736 (industrial) and AS 5810 (home) certification through the Australasian Bioplastics Association is voluntary but expected for "compostable" claims. Under Australian Consumer Law, unverified green claims are a risk (ACCC greenwashing enforcement, general knowledge). Expect a BICON check under "plant material for manufacturing or processed plant products". Inspection on arrival and possible treatment are common for plant-fibre goods.

### Gaps
- ABA certification fees and timelines. The search found nothing.
- The specific BICON case for areca leaf tableware.
- The Korean category for natural leaf utensils and its test items.
- Japan quarantine-station test items for natural plant utensils.

## Q4. Vietnam domestic: QCVN, food safety self-declaration, Vietnam Green Label

### Takeaway
Under Decree 15/2018/NĐ-CP, food containers and food-contact packaging are subject to **self-declaration (tự công bố)**. The self-declaration uses Form 01, backed by a test report from a designated lab, valid 12 months. The QCVN 12-x series covers plastic (12-1:2011), rubber (12-2:2011), metal (12-3:2011) and glass/ceramic (12-4:2015). **There is no QCVN for plant or leaf materials.** Testing is therefore based on general safety criteria or the target market's standards. A replacement decree for 15/2018 was being drafted in 2025 under the amended Food Safety Law, and its final status is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Article 4 of Decree 15/2018 puts food containers and packaging in direct contact with food under self-declaration. The dossier is in Article 5 (Form No. 01, Appendix I). — [Decree 15/2018 (EN)](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/EN/The-thao-Y-te/Decree-15-2018-ND-CP-elaboration-law-of-food-safety/375807/tieng-anh.aspx); [Decree 15/2018 (VI)](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/the-thao-y-te/nghi-dinh-15-2018-nd-cp-huong-dan-luat-an-toan-thuc-pham-341254.aspx?temp=d)
- Applicable QCVN: 12-1:2011/BYT (plastic), 12-2:2011/BYT (rubber), 12-3:2011/BYT (metal), 12-4:2015/BYT (glass, ceramic, enamel). — [ACC Group guide](https://accgroup.vn/cong-bo-bao-bi-chua-dung-thuc-pham)
- In 2025 the Ministry of Health drafted a decree to replace 15/2018. Commenters flagged inconsistencies, such as registration versus conformity declaration. One draft proposed a 2-year transition for existing self-declaration dossiers. — [Gov. consultation summary Sep 2025](https://datafiles.chinhphu.vn/cpp/files/duthaovbpl/2025/Thang9/3.5.-ban-tong-hop-ttgt-dn-hh-nd-thay-the-03.9.2025.pdf); [Comment summary May 2025](https://datafiles.chinhphu.vn/cpp/files/duthaovbpl/2025/Thang9/3.3.-ban-tong-hop-y-kien-1-so-hh-du-thao-4-26.5.2025-ngoc.pdf)

### Inferences
- In practice, a domestic self-declaration for mo cau products could use test items modelled on QCVN 12-1 general criteria (heavy metals Pb/Cd, KMnO4 consumption, evaporation residue) plus microbiology and mould. Test labs designated by the Ministry of Health or accredited to ISO/IEC 17025 (for example Quatest 3, Pasteur Institute HCMC, SGS Vietnam, Eurofins) typically quote a few million VND per sample (general knowledge, unverified).
- "Nhãn Xanh Việt Nam" (Vietnam Green Label), run by the Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment (MONRE, now the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment after the 2025 merger), is voluntary. The set of criteria for disposable tableware or bio-packaging should be checked (general knowledge, unverified; not found in search).
- Exports themselves do not need a Vietnamese self-declaration unless the product is also sold domestically. The buyer's market sets the requirements. Plant quarantine: the PPD (Plant Protection Department, regional sub-departments) issues phytosanitary certificates on request. Fumigation by a licensed provider (ISPM 15 applies only to wooden pallets, not the product) costs a few million VND per container (general knowledge, unverified).

### Gaps
- Whether the replacement decree for 15/2018 was issued in 2026 and what its new classification is.
- Vietnam Green Label criteria for disposable food-contact products.
- Official fees and lead times for Vietnamese self-declaration testing.

## Q5. Other: FSC, ISO 22000/HACCP/BRCGS Packaging, test costs, phytosanitary/fumigation, heavy metals/mould

### Takeaway
FSC certifies forest-based products. Areca leaf sheath falls under neither FSC's wood nor its NTFP (non-timber forest product) categories in most cases, because it is a fallen agricultural by-product from plantations. It is not a priority. For EU and Japanese retail and food-service buyers, a food-safety management system matters more: BRCGS Packaging Materials (Issue 7), FSSC 22000 or ISO 22000. Mould control (moisture content under about 10-12% and proper storage) is the main recurring quality complaint. Published lab price lists were not found.

### Cited Findings
- For EU imports, fumigation or heat treatment is only a condition for specific regulated pests and products under 2019/2072. — [ComplianceGate](https://compliancegate.com/fumigation-regulations-european-union); [EC plant health](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/trade-plants-plant-products-non-eu-countries_en)
- BEUC testing found contaminants of concern in natural-fibre disposable tableware, including palm leaf. This supports testing for heavy metals, pesticides and other contaminants. — [BEUC/vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/sites/default/files/2021-05/Report_BEUC_Towards%20safe%20and%20sustainable%20FCM_0.pdf)

### Inferences (general knowledge, unverified; obtain quotes)
- BRCGS Packaging certification: an audit by a certification body is roughly USD 3,000-6,000 per year plus consultancy, with 4-8 months to get ready. ISO 22000 or HACCP costs similar or less. Many EU distributors accept an ISO 22000 or HACCP certificate plus test reports for small importers. Large retailers often require BRCGS.
- FSC: possibly relevant only if paper sleeves or cardboard cartons are used in the outer packaging. FSC-certified cartons can then be sourced, which needs CoC only if the FSC claim is made on the product.
- Test set for a "global" product dossier (repeat every 12 months or when the process changes):
  1. Heavy metals (Pb, Cd, Hg, As, Cr) in material plus migration (4% acetic acid).
  2. Overall migration (EU 10 mg/dm², as a reference).
  3. Sensory testing (DIN 10955).
  4. Mould and yeast, total plate count.
  5. Pesticide residue screen.
  6. PFAS: total fluorine or TOF, then targeted (PPWR, US states).
  7. Formaldehyde, if any adhesive or binder is used.
  8. **Areca alkaloids (arecoline, arecaidine, guvacoline, guvacine) migration.** This is a new topic since the FDA action. It is not a routine test, and few labs may have a validated method, so ask SGS or Eurofins.
  9. Microwave and oven suitability, only if claimed.
  10. EN 13432 or ASTM D6400/D6868 compostability, only for compostability claims.
- Labelling and claims rules to remember:
  - EU Green Claims and ECGT (Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition) Directive 2024/825 applies from 27 Sep 2026. It bans generic claims such as "eco-friendly" or "biodegradable" without recognised proof, and bans sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme (general knowledge, unverified; verify).
  - PPWR harmonised labelling (material pictograms) from 2028 or later.
  - The food-contact "glass and fork" symbol or the words "for food contact" under Art. 15 of 1935/2004.
  - Washington and California restrict compostable claims and colours.
  - Avoid "biodegradable" alone in the US (FTC Green Guides; banned in CA for plastics).
